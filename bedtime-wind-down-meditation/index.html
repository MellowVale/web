A five-minute bedtime wind-down meditation

Try this five-minute practice when you’re ready to close out the day. You can sit on the edge of your bed or lie down and keep your eyes open or closed.

5 minutes · In bed or in a comfortable chair

Listen to the guided step ↓

Follow the five-minute wind-down

  1. Get comfortable

    Let your hands rest. Spend the first minute feeling where the bed, chair, or floor supports you.

  2. Find one point of focus

    For another minute, stay with a quiet sound, the feeling of your head on the pillow, or the movement of a natural breath.

  3. Set a thought aside

    If a task from the day comes to mind, name it in a few words. Try “the email” or “tomorrow’s appointment.” Then return to what you can feel. If your body is restless, settle your attention in one place.

    What’s keeping you company tonight?

    If your thoughts turn into a list

    When the next task comes to mind, feel the pillow under your cheek. Give that soft spot one more breath.

    If you keep shifting under the covers

    Let the blanket settle over one shoulder. Find one place that already feels comfortable. Rest your attention there.

  4. Widen your attention

    For the last two minutes, notice the room around you and return to the feeling of being supported. When you’re ready to finish, take a moment before reaching for your phone or getting up.

Make this part of your evening

Pick a cue you already recognize, such as putting your book down or switching off the main light. On a night when five minutes feels long, spend one minute on the first step.

If the room is noisy, use one steady point of contact, such as your hands on the blanket. When your attention wanders, feel that point again and continue from where you are.
