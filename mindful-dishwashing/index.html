Mindful dishwashing, one cup at a time

The dishes are still on the table, and part of you is already halfway through tomorrow. Pick one cup from dinner. For this small evening practice, follow it from your hands to the drying rack.

A minute or two · At the sink or kitchen table

Listen or copy a check-in ↓

Stay with the cup

  1. Feel where your hands meet it

    Pick a sturdy mug or bowl. Notice the weight before it fills with water. Run your thumb across the glaze and the unglazed ring underneath. Even a cup you use every day may feel a little different there.

  2. Listen while you rinse

    Wash at your usual pace. Listen as the stream lands inside the cup, then as it hits the empty sink. If you find yourself rehearsing tomorrow’s conversation, feel the handle in your hand again.

  3. Hear it come to rest

    Turn off the tap. As you dry the cup, notice where the towel catches and where it slides. Put the cup down and listen for the little sound it makes. That is the end of the practice. You can wash the rest any way you like.

If someone else is washing

Sit at the table with a dry towel and a rinsed dish. Turn the dish once in your hands as you dry it. Or take a clean towel from the laundry and fold it, noticing how the thick hem feels different from the cloth in the middle.

If someone speaks to you, put the dish down and listen. When you pick it up again, start with the feel of its weight. There is no timer to restart.
